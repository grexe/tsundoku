- **Say what it is.** You may use the names to talk about the software, to say that your work is compatible with it or works

- **Redistribute the unmodified software** under its own name, for instance in a package repository or a distribution (SENryu,

## What you may not do

- Sell, distribute or promote a **modified version or a fork under the same name**, or under a name that can be confused with it
  (a different spelling, "Tsundoku Pro", "SEN Reader"). Give a fork its own name and say that it is based on the original.
- Suggest that your product or service **comes from or is endorsed by** SEN Labs when it is not.
- Register the names, or names that are confusingly similar, as a trademark, a domain name or an account or package name for
  software of the same kind.

A fork that **keeps its changes small and says so** ("Tsundoku, patched for X") is fine for a package that is built and shipped
for a distribution, as long as the changes are listed.

## Why

People who install "Tsundoku" or anything called "SEN" should get what the name promises. The names say that the software is
the one SEN Labs maintains, with its attributes, annotations and behavior. This is how Debian, Mozilla and Haiku treat their own
names, too.
